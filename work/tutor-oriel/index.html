Problem

Tutor Oriel matches students with tutors: create an account, answer four questions, book and pay. I designed both flows, from interviews to a clickable prototype.

Sign-up lost people; course selection lost their trust. The form stated no rules and caught no typos. The course list named subjects, not tutors.

A student is buying an hour with a stranger. They need the right information at the moment they choose.

Goals
  • Fewer drop-offs, faster sign-up.
  • A stronger, more readable brand.
  • Functions easy to find and use.

Research

Interviews, then a usability test on the mid-fi prototype: one person, one task, watching where they stopped.

Every request was about the tutor, not the subject.

Decisions

One change per leak, small enough to test.

ChangeWhy
Create an account
Email fieldThe booking arrives by mail.

Confirmation fieldTypos caught early.

Course selection
Background and experience on the cardJudge the tutor, not the subject.
More subjectsThe list was too thin.
Return, cancel, reschedule on every stepNo dead ends.
Date and location inside the flowNothing left to arrange.
Online or in personChosen, not assumed.

Design

Three screens carry the flow: register, choose a tutor, pay. Green on grey and white, Roboto throughout.

Outcome

A clickable prototype of the whole path, never built.

If it were, I would watch drop-off per step, completion from account to booking, and time to first booking.

Reflection

Start from where people stop, not from the brief. Each round changed only what the last test exposed.
